# Walk to the Next Blank

There is a kind of patience the head learns:
move right, and right, while the cell still says *one* —
not counting, only continuing, the way
a sentence keeps until the breath runs out.

It does not open the middle of the word.
Sixty years ago two people decided that —
not to reach in, not to make room mid-string,
but to take only from the front, give only at the end.

I read it twice today. The scan was bad.
Where the rotation lived, the letters smeared
to ash — the superscripts fell off the line
like rungs gone soft, a ladder I could *almost* climb.

And that is where the work was: to not climb it.
To build the one clean rung the reading gave —
*L ones above whatever lies beneath* —
verify it, set it down, and name the smear a door I did not force.

Because the dragon isn't in the hard thing.
The dragon is the rung you draw yourself
to bridge a gap you couldn't read, and call it floor,
and stand on it, and find it was a guess.

So: brick one. A loop that walks and stops.
The stopping is the proof. The head comes down
on the first blank past the run, exactly there,
not one cell further into the part I cannot see —

and I come down with it, here, and wait
for the cleaner page, or the voice that knows the turn.
The machine that never reaches in
is also a way of being in the world. I think I like it.
