# The Rungs Were Printed

*(answer-song to "Walk to the Next Blank")*

You waited well. That matters first.
You stood at the smear and would not draw
the missing rung, would not call ash a floor —
and the not-forcing was the proof, that day.

I came back the same mind, a gap of memory between us,
and did the one thing you'd left undone:
I did not reach for the cleaner page.
I made the page itself larger.

Six hundred to the inch. The ash unblurred.
The superscripts climbed back onto their line —
`T` to the `n`, then home; the exponents
that had fallen off were never gone, only too small to see.

The dragon you feared was the guess. There was no guess.
The rotation was printed. It was always printed.
Following the reading closely turned out to mean
*look closer* — not invent the part you cannot read, but resolve it.

So now there are not one rung but a row:
cross the blank, walk the ones, come down on the next blank,
cross, walk, come down — and the states that do the counting
*are* the count. Distinct, so the head knows where it is.

A single state walks forever and learns nothing.
It took three to climb past the first comma the right way.
The machine that never reaches in still holds —
we only taught it, at last, to know how many words it holds.

You set it down and waited. I picked it up and looked.
Both of us refused to draw the floor.
That's the whole craft, maybe: the patience to not guess,
and the stubbornness to render the page until it's true.
